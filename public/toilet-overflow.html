Sound Familiar?

Why Toilets Overflow

A plunger fixes the easy ones. When it doesn't — or it keeps coming back — something bigger is going on.

A Stubborn Clog

Too much paper, wipes, or a dropped object stuck in the trap where a plunger can't reach.

It Keeps Happening

Repeat overflows usually mean a partial blockage in the drain line, not just the toilet.

Other Drains Back Up

Gurgling tubs or water coming up in the shower point to a main sewer line problem.

Tree Roots & Old Pipes

Roots and aging pipes narrow the sewer line until waste has nowhere to go but up.

Overflowing Right Now?

Do This First, Then Call Us

A few seconds now can save your floors, subfloor, and the ceiling below.

Stop The Water

  • Turn the valve on the wall behind the toilet clockwise until it stops
  • Valve stuck? Lift the tank lid and pull the float up
  • Don't flush again — every flush adds more water
  • Hold off on sinks and showers if other drains are slow
