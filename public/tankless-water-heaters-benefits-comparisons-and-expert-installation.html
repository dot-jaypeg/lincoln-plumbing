Tankless Water Heaters: Benefits, Comparisons, and Expert Installation

Are you tired of running out of hot water mid-shower or seeing high energy bills? Tankless water heaters, also known as on-demand water heaters, might be the perfect solution. Unlike traditional systems that store hot water in large tanks, tankless water heaters heat water as it flows through the unit, providing an endless supply of hot water when you need it. Below, we’ll discuss the benefits of tankless water heaters, how they compare to traditional systems, and when to call a professional for installation and maintenance.

Why Choose a Tankless Water Heater?

Tankless water heaters have become a popular choice for homeowners seeking efficiency, reliability, and space-saving designs.

Key Benefits

  • Energy Efficiency: Traditional water heaters heat and reheat water in a storage tank, leading to energy loss. Tankless systems heat water only when needed, saving energy and reducing utility bills. According to the U.S. Department of Energy, tankless water heaters are 24–34% more energy-efficient for homes that use up to 41 gallons of water daily.
  • Endless Hot Water: With a tankless water heater, you never run out of hot water. This is particularly helpful for larger households where hot water demand is high.
  • Compact Size: Tankless units are smaller than traditional water heaters, freeing up valuable space. They can even be wall-mounted for additional convenience.
  • Longer Lifespan: While traditional water heaters typically last 10–15 years, tankless models can last up to 20 years with proper maintenance, making them a long-term investment.

Tankless Water Heaters vs. Traditional Water Heaters

Before upgrading to a tankless system, it’s important to understand how they compare to traditional water heaters in terms of performance, installation, and maintenance.

Size and Installation

  • Traditional Water Heaters: These systems require large tanks, often taking up considerable space in basements or utility rooms.
  • Tankless Water Heaters: Compact and versatile, tankless units can be installed in tight spaces, such as closets or on walls. However, installation can be more complex, especially when switching from a traditional system to a tankless one.

Energy Savings and Performance

  • Traditional Water Heaters: Constantly heating stored water can waste energy, especially if you don’t use hot water frequently.
  • Tankless Water Heaters: These systems eliminate standby heat loss by only heating water as it’s needed, improving energy efficiency and reducing costs.

Maintenance and Lifespan

  • Traditional Water Heaters: Require periodic flushing to remove sediment buildup and generally last 10–15 years.
  • Tankless Water Heaters: Require annual descaling to prevent mineral buildup, particularly in areas with hard water. With proper care, these systems can last up to 20 years.

When to Hire a Professional Plumber for Installation

While tankless water heaters offer numerous benefits, proper installation is essential to ensure optimal performance.

Why Professional Installation Matters

  • Safety: Tankless water heaters involve gas or electric connections, which require specialized knowledge to install safely. Incorrect installation can result in gas leaks, electrical issues, or water damage.
  • Efficiency: A professional plumber will ensure your tankless system is correctly sized for your household’s water demand and connected properly to avoid performance issues.
  • Warranty Protection: Many manufacturers require professional installation to maintain warranty coverage.

Signs It’s Time for Professional Help

  • You’re converting from a traditional water heater to a tankless system.
  • You’re unsure how to size the unit for your home’s water demand.
  • You want to ensure safe and efficient installation of gas or electric connections.

Maintaining Your Tankless Water Heater

To keep your tankless water heater running efficiently, regular maintenance is crucial.

Descaling and Cleaning

Over time, mineral buildup can affect the performance of your tankless water heater, particularly in areas with hard water. Descaling removes these deposits and ensures your system continues to operate efficiently.

Steps for Descaling:

  1. Turn off the power and water supply to the unit.
  2. Attach a descaling pump to the service valves.
  3. Run a descaling solution through the unit for 45–60 minutes.
  4. Flush the system with clean water to remove residue.

Annual Inspections

Schedule annual maintenance with a professional plumber to ensure all components are functioning correctly. Regular inspections can catch small issues before they turn into costly repairs.

Why a Tankless Water Heater Is Worth the Investment

Tankless water heaters provide a continuous hot water supply, improved energy efficiency, and a longer lifespan compared to traditional systems. While DIY solutions may seem tempting, professional installation and maintenance are key to getting the most out of your investment.
