Why Toilets Overflow

A plunger fixes the easy ones. When it doesn't, or it keeps coming back, something bigger is going on.

Overflowing Toilet FAQ

What should I do when my toilet is overflowing?

Turn the shut-off valve on the wall behind the toilet clockwise until the water stops, and don't flush again. If the valve won't turn, lift the tank lid and raise the float to stop the fill, or shut off your home's main water. Then call us at 909-765-0236.

Why does my toilet keep overflowing?

Most overflows come from a clog in the toilet or the drain line right below it. If it keeps happening, or other drains gurgle or back up at the same time, the blockage is often further down in the main sewer line. We find the actual cause before recommending any work, and can run a camera down the line so you can see it too.

Is an overflowing toilet a plumbing emergency?

If the water won't stop, or sewage is coming up into a tub, shower or floor drain, treat it as an emergency. We're open 24 hours a day, 7 days a week. Call 909-765-0236 any time.
