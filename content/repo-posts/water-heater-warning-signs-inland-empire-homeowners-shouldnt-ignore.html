Your water heater is one of those appliances that tends to go unnoticed — until something goes wrong. For most homeowners in the Inland Empire, a water heater runs quietly in the background for years, and it's easy to assume it will keep doing so indefinitely. But like any mechanical system, water heaters wear down over time. Knowing the early warning signs can be the difference between a simple repair and an emergency replacement.

Your Hot Water Isn't as Hot as It Used to Be

If you've noticed your showers getting lukewarm faster than they used to, or the water never quite reaches the temperature you're expecting, your water heater may be struggling. This is often caused by a failing heating element in electric units or a faulty burner in gas systems. Sediment buildup at the bottom of the tank can also reduce heating efficiency over time. In the Inland Empire, where hard water is common, mineral deposits accumulate faster than in other regions, making this a particularly relevant concern for local homeowners.

Discolored or Rusty Water

Rusty or brownish water coming from your hot water tap is a sign that corrosion may be occurring inside your tank. While rust in the water line can sometimes be the culprit, discoloration that only appears with hot water typically points to the tank itself. Once a water heater begins corroding from the inside, it's usually nearing the end of its useful life. Left unaddressed, a corroded tank can eventually develop leaks that cause significant water damage to your home.

Strange Noises Coming from the Tank

A water heater that pops, rumbles, or bangs during operation is trying to tell you something. These sounds are most often caused by hardened sediment at the bottom of the tank being disturbed as the water heats up. Over time, this sediment layer forces the unit to work harder, driving up energy costs and accelerating wear. If you're hearing unusual sounds from your unit, it's worth having a plumber take a look before the problem gets worse.

Water Pooling Around the Base of the Unit

Any moisture or standing water around your water heater is a red flag. Even small amounts of water near the base of the unit could indicate a leak in the tank, a loose connection, or a failing pressure relief valve. Some of these issues are repairable, but a cracked or leaking tank typically means it's time for a replacement. Either way, this is not something to put off — water damage spreads quickly and can affect flooring, walls, and nearby structures.

Your Water Heater Is Getting Up There in Age

Most traditional tank water heaters have a lifespan of 8 to 12 years. If yours is approaching or past that range, it's worth scheduling a professional inspection even if you haven't noticed obvious symptoms. Older units are more prone to sudden failure, and proactive replacement on your own schedule is far less disruptive than an unexpected breakdown — especially during colder months when hot water demand is highest.
